Do the generated fields have the flow's statistics, and are they copies?¶

An added experiment on the trained denoiser of Stage 1 (40 epochs, a single training: it takes longer than 15 minutes, so it is not repeated). Every statistic is computed against the 577 held-out real fields the denoiser never saw (Stage 1's validation split, default_rng(0)), never against the training fields.

  • Part (a): statistics. Mean and RMS profiles of u across the channel, the streamwise energy spectrum, the streamwise two-point correlation at y+ ≈ 15 and at the centre, and the probability density of u at y+ ≈ 15, for the diffusion samples (Stage 1's 200-step DDPM sampler), the Gaussian baseline of Stage 1's Exercise 1 (independent pixels with the mean and RMS profiles of the training fields) and the held-out real fields. The training fields are shown as a second real reference: their difference from the held-out fields is the natural sampling difference between two sets of real fields.
  • Part (b): copying and cost. For every generated sample and every held-out real field, the distance to the nearest of the 5,200 training fields; the two distributions side by side. Then the cost of sampling against quality for 200-step DDPM and 50-, 20- and 10-step DDIM.

Three sampling seeds (0, 1, 2) of 256 samples each for every sampler; the Gaussian baseline also with three seeds.

Stage 2 — Field statistics and memorisation¶

Portfolio project: are the generated fields turbulent, and are they new?

Stage 1 checks the generator on the mean and standard-deviation profiles of 64 samples against all fields, the training fields included. Here I hold it to stricter terms, using the trained denoiser of Stage 1 (no new training). Every statistic is computed against the 577 fields that the denoiser never saw: mean and RMS profiles, the streamwise energy spectrum, the two-point correlation near the wall and at the centre, and the probability density of u at y+ ≈ 15. The generated fields are compared with the simplest generator a careful engineer would try first, independent Gaussian pixels with the training fields' profiles. Then I ask whether the model copies its training set: how close is each generated field to its nearest training field, compared with how close the held-out real fields are? Finally, I weigh the cost of sampling against quality for 200-step DDPM and 50-, 20- and 10-step DDIM. Every sampler is run with three noise seeds of 256 samples each.

def sample(n, seed, method="ddpm", steps=50, chunk=64):
    """Stage 1's DDPM update, or deterministic DDIM (eta = 0) as in its Exercise 3; physical, cropped."""
    b = beta.astype(np.float64); a = 1.0 - b; ab = np.cumprod(a); gen = tf.random.Generator.from_seed(seed); out = []
    for s in range(0, n, chunk):
        m = min(chunk, n - s); x = gen.normal((m, n_y_padded, n_x, 1))
        if method == "ddpm":
            for t in reversed(range(T)):
                eps = eps_fn(x, tf.fill((m,), float(t)))
                mean = (x - float(b[t] / np.sqrt(1 - ab[t])) * eps) / float(np.sqrt(a[t]))
                x = mean + float(np.sqrt(b[t])) * gen.normal(tf.shape(x)) if t > 0 else mean
        else:
            ts = np.unique(np.linspace(0, T - 1, steps).round().astype(int))[::-1]
            for i, t in enumerate(ts):
                eps = eps_fn(x, tf.fill((m,), float(t)))
                x0 = (x - float(np.sqrt(1 - ab[t])) * eps) / float(np.sqrt(ab[t]))
                abp = ab[ts[i + 1]] if i + 1 < len(ts) else 1.0
                x = float(np.sqrt(abp)) * x0 + float(np.sqrt(1 - abp)) * eps
        out.append(x.numpy()[:, :n_y_raw, :, 0] * data_std + data_mean)
    return np.concatenate(out)
_ = eps_fn(tf.zeros((64, n_y_padded, n_x, 1)), tf.zeros((64,)))   # warm-up

def spectrum(u):
    up = u - u.mean(axis=(0, 2), keepdims=True); acc = np.zeros((u.shape[1], n_x // 2 + 1))
    for s in range(0, len(u), 64): acc += (np.abs(np.fft.rfft(up[s:s + 64], axis=-1)) ** 2).sum(0)
    return acc / len(u) / n_x ** 2
def corr_x(u, j):
    up = u[:, j, :] - u[:, j, :].mean(); P = (np.abs(np.fft.rfft(up, axis=-1)) ** 2).mean(0); R = np.fft.irfft(P, n=n_x); return (R / R[0])[: n_x // 2 + 1]
def first_below(R, lv=0.5):
    k = np.where(R < lv)[0]; return (k[0] if len(k) else len(R)) * dx
BINS = np.linspace(min(held[:, j15].min(), train[:, j15].min()) - 0.05, max(held[:, j15].max(), train[:, j15].max()) + 0.05, 61)
def pdf(u): h, _ = np.histogram(u[:, j15, :].ravel(), bins=BINS, density=True); return h

def stats(u):

    def lsd(rows, k1=1, k2=KMAX): return float(np.sqrt(np.mean(np.log10(E[rows][:, k1:k2 + 1].mean(0) / REF["E"][rows][:, k1:k2 + 1].mean(0)) ** 2)))

Part (b): is it copying the training set, and what does sampling cost?¶

Distance of a field to the training set = RMS difference in u (stored units, over the 201 × 384 points) to the nearest of the 5,200 training fields. If the generator copied training fields, its samples would sit much closer to the training set than held-out real fields do (and held-out fields include neighbouring planes of training snapshots, so their distances are small to begin with).
